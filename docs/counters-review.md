# Counter and timer usability review

Reviewed Poker, Uruguayan Truco, La Polilla, Coup and Skull King using their setup, active play, correction, completion and reset flows. The existing scoring models generally fit their stated purposes. The main issues were the amount of scrolling during play, unclear feedback, and inconsistent recovery after reloading.

| Tool | Finding | Improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poker | A tournament blind timer makes sense, but the full disabled schedule crowded the active timer. Reset immediately discarded timing progress. Validation did not identify the field to fix. | Collapse the schedule on start, retain access through its summary, show progress and a distinct paused state, explain what previous/next do, confirm reset, and highlight/focus invalid inputs. |
| Truco | Manual points are appropriate because local bidding conventions differ. On phones, both scores appeared before either team's controls, making it easy to lose track of the target team. | Put each team's total, phase and award buttons in the same card. Move undo above history. Recover saved scores, target and corrected/excluded history after reload. |
| La Polilla | Card-count penalties and lowest-total standings fit the game. Explicitly entering every zero was tedious, and penalties appeared only after a separate preview. Duplicate names could make the empty-hand selection ambiguous. | Show each hand's penalty while entering it; add an explicit shortcut to fill only blank fields with zero; require distinct names; retain preview/confirmation and deck limits; recover confirmed rounds after reload. |
| Coup | A session scoreboard is useful when labeled as house scoring. It was below all the quick rules, and a player could be selected as both winner and runner-up. | Put the scoreboard first, fold the full points explanation into help, preview the award, disable the winning player in the runner-up list and clear a conflicting selection when the winner changes. Report unavailable storage. |
| Skull King | Classic scoring and a separate explained adjustment support the intended edition. Optional adjustment fields cluttered every player card, ignored bonuses were insufficiently explained, and there was no last-round undo. | Fold optional adjustments, explain bonus eligibility beside the input, reject more won tricks than dealt cards while allowing fewer, add undo that restores the round's entries, require distinct names and recover saved rounds after reload. |

Detailed help remains available in English and Spanish. Touch controls retain a minimum height of 44px. Corrections, completed-game results and explicit resets remain available. Interactive tools stay out of printed rule references. Both builders regenerate the same standalone offline artifact.

Rule scope was checked against the [publisher's Skull King rules and FAQ](https://www.grandpabecksgames.com/pages/skull-king) and [Schmidt Spiele's Cheating Moth rules](https://www.schmidtspiele.de/files/Produkte/4/40862%20-%20Mogel%20Motte/40862_Mogelmotte_DE_GB_FR_IT.pdf), as well as the existing guide and scoring tests. Coup's session points remain house scoring; Truco does not infer points from cards or bids. Skull King still supports classic scoring with manual optional effects, including Graybeard and destroyed tricks as reasons for fewer recorded tricks than dealt cards.

Validation uses the five existing game suites plus `tests/scoreboard-ux.cjs`. The additional suite exercises all five tools in both HTML entry points at 320, 390 and 1440px in both languages, and checks reloads, previews, storage failure feedback, the position of Coup's scoreboard, control grouping, and undo. Screenshots of active mobile and desktop flows are generated for visual review. The general site suite checks offline operation, navigation, responsive layouts, script syntax and build parity.

Saved results use this browser's local storage. Unsubmitted entries survive guide, language and theme switches while the page is open, but are not recovered after reload. Saving does not sync between devices or separate site origins. Sound still requires activation after each page load. Browser checks do not replace physical-device or assistive-technology testing, and this was a usability review rather than a study with players.
